Milin Patel

Research Associate & PhD Candidate | Safety, Cybersecurity & AI for Automated Driving

Prodigy in an infant state on career progression path.

I am an engineer and researcher at the Institute for Driver Assistance and Connected Mobility (IFM) of Kempten University of Applied Sciences, Germany. I work on the safety and cybersecurity of artificial intelligence (AI) in automated driving.

Some vehicles can drive parts of a journey without the driver. For this, software has to recognise people, other vehicles and the road from sensor data. This software uses AI and can make errors, for example in heavy rain or when someone manipulates a sensor.

I develop methods to find such errors, before the release of a vehicle and during its operation, and to demonstrate to an independent assessor that the remaining risk is acceptable. The methods are simulation-based tests, performance measurements for AI-based perception, and assurance cases that combine safety and cybersecurity.

Areas of work

Performance of AI-based perception

I measure how well object detection performs in adverse weather, with LiDAR (laser-based distance sensor) data generated in simulation, and I evaluate the uncertainty of each detection.

Result: a simulated LiDAR dataset with 21 weather conditions, and an evaluation in which disagreement between several detectors separates incorrect detections from correct ones.

Uncertainty evaluation · uncertainty representation · performance evaluation

Safety and cybersecurity assurance

An assurance case is a structured argument, supported by evidence, that a system is acceptably safe. I combine the safety claims and the cybersecurity claims for an AI-based perception component in one assurance case.

Result: at the verification and validation goal, the evidence types of the standards use different measurement scales, and no standard prescribes how they combine.

Assurance argument pattern · assurance case explorer

Driver and automation

I test reasonably foreseeable misuse of automated driving functions by the driver in simulation.

Result: test requirements, evaluation criteria, and a procedure to evaluate measures that prevent or mitigate misuse.

Testing of foreseeable misuse · mitigation of foreseeable misuse

Scenario-based testing

I define driving scenarios and plan trajectories for the verification and validation of automated driving functions in simulation.

Result: a lane change trajectory planned with a high-order polynomial, analysed in simulation, with a public Simulink model.

Trajectory planning · transverse guidance · model

Literature review

I reviewed the published research on the safety of the intended functionality (SOTIF), which covers hazards that arise without a fault.

Result: the research gaps and a comparison of the methods that support SOTIF.

Systematic literature review

Education

I supervise bachelor's and master's theses and conduct workshops for students from European countries in the Erasmus+ programme.

Result: a public school workshop on AI-based perception and two interactive explanations of my research.
